The straight answer: **you probably cannot get the 2004 box holder’s real name directly from USPS unless you have a legal reason.** USPS treats PO Box customer information as protected. Current federal rules say PO Box holder information from PS Form 1093 is released only in limited cases, such as to a government agency, someone serving legal process, an attorney, a self represented party in litigation, or by subpoena or court order. It is not a general public lookup. ([eCFR][1])

For **PO Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327**, here are the best lawful routes:

### 1. If this is for a lawsuit or legal service

Use the USPS **Change of Address or Boxholder Information Needed for Service of Legal Process** request. USPS says these requests should go directly to the **Postmaster of the last known address**, not through a normal FOIA request. ([pfoiapal.usps.com][2])

Mail it to:

**Postmaster**
**Dunlap Post Office**
**15198 Rankin Ave**
**Dunlap, TN 37327-9998**

USPS lists the Dunlap Post Office at that address. ([tools.usps.com][3])

On the form, use:

**Address:** PO Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327

The USPS form says the **name, if known, and the Post Office box address** are required for box holder information, and that there is no fee. It also warns that using the form falsely or for anything other than service of legal process can bring criminal penalties. ([About USPS][4])

### 2. Understand the 2004 problem

USPS may not still have the old record. A 2004 USPS retention schedule listed **PS Form 1093, Application for Post Office Box or Caller Service**, as a **2 year** record. ([About USPS][5]) A later USPS notice says PS Form 1093 should be retained **2 years after the box is closed**. ([About USPS][6])

So if PO Box 190 was closed years ago, USPS likely no longer has the 2004 application. If the same box stayed active, USPS may only have current or recent information, not necessarily who had it in 2004.
