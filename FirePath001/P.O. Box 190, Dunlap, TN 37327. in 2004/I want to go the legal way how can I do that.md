
I want to go the legal way how can I do that? 

The legal way is **not FOIA** and not a public records lookup. For USPS, you use the **Change of Address or Boxholder Request Format for service of legal process**, or you get a **subpoena or court order**.

USPS says process servers and government agencies should send address information requests **directly to the Postmaster of the last known address**, and that these requests **should not be submitted as FOIA requests**. ([PFOIA Pal][1])

## What you need first

You need a real legal purpose tied to **service of legal process**. That means you are trying to serve someone as a defendant, witness, respondent, or similar role in an actual or prospective court matter. USPS regulations allow PO Box applicant name and address information from PS Form 1093 only in limited cases, including to a person legally allowed to serve process, an attorney for a party, or a party acting **pro se**, meaning representing yourself. ([eCFR][2])

The hard truth: you cannot honestly use this process just because you are curious. The USPS form warns that false information, or using the information for anything other than service of legal process in actual or prospective litigation, can bring criminal penalties. ([USPS][3])

## Step 1: Use the USPS boxholder request form

Use the USPS form titled:

**Request for Change of Address or Boxholder Information Needed for Service of Legal Process**

The form asks for the PO Box address, your capacity, the parties, the court, the docket number if one exists, and whether the person is being served as a defendant, witness, or something else. USPS says one request may be made per completed form, and there is no fee for boxholder or change of address information. ([USPS][3])

For your request, write it like this:

```text
Postmaster
Dunlap Post Office
15198 Rankin Ave
Dunlap, TN 37327-9998

Re: Request for Boxholder Information Needed for Service of Legal Process

Name: Unknown boxholder, if not known
Address: P.O. Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327

Please provide the boxholder applicant name and street address recorded for P.O. Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327, for the 2004 time period, if records are available, for service of legal process.
```

The Dunlap Post Office is listed by USPS at **15198 Rankin Ave, Dunlap, TN 37327-9998**. ([USPS Tools][4])

## Step 2: Fill out the six required legal fields

On the USPS form:

1. **Capacity of requester**
   Write one of these:

   ```text
   Party representing self
   ```

   or

   ```text
   Attorney for plaintiff
   ```

   or

   ```text
   Process server
   ```

2. **Statute or regulation that empowers you to serve process**
   If you are an attorney or representing yourself, the USPS form says this is not required, except for a corporation acting pro se. ([USPS][3])
   If this is a Tennessee civil case and you are using a process server, Tennessee Rule of Civil Procedure 4.01 says a summons and complaint may be served by a person who is not a party and is at least 18 years old. ([Tennessee Courts][5])

3. **Names of all known parties**
   Example:

   ```text
   [Your name] v. Unknown boxholder of P.O. Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327
   ```

4. **Court where the case has been or will be heard**
   Example:

   ```text
   Sequatchie County General Sessions Court
   ```

   Use the real court that applies to your matter.

5. **Docket or identifying number**
   Use the case number if you already filed. If you have not filed yet, check the option saying no docket number has been issued.

6. **Capacity in which the individual is to be served**
   Example:

   ```text
   Defendant
   ```

   or

   ```text
   Witness
   ```

Then sign and date the certification.

## Step 3: Send it to the Dunlap Postmaster

Mail it with tracking and keep a copy of everything:

```text
Postmaster
Dunlap Post Office
15198 Rankin Ave
Dunlap, TN 37327-9998
```

I would send it by Certified Mail with return receipt so you can prove it was received.

## Step 4: Be ready for a “no record” answer

Because you are asking about **2004**, the record may be gone. USPS Postal Bulletin guidance says PS Form 1093 and PS Form 1093 C should be retained for **2 years after the box is closed**. ([USPS][6])

So the legal request may come back with:

```text
No record
No such address
No change of address order on file
Moved, left no forwarding address
```

or, if the record still exists and the request is valid, the boxholder name and street address.

Also, USPS regulations say that under this legal process route, USPS may provide the boxholder information, but **a copy of PS Form 1093 itself will not be provided**. ([eCFR][2])

## Step 5: If USPS refuses or says they need a court order

Your next legal route is a subpoena or court order. USPS says subpoenas or other requests should be mailed to:

```text
Office of General Counsel
United States Postal Service
475 L’Enfant Plaza SW
Washington, DC 20260-1100
```

USPS also says a Postal Service employee may not produce records or give testimony without prior authorization from the General Counsel. ([USPS][7]) ([eCFR][8])

The most practical path is this:

File the case if you have a valid claim, get a case number, ask the clerk or an attorney how to issue a subpoena or motion for court order, then direct that subpoena or order to USPS for any retained records showing the boxholder for **P.O. Box 190, Dunlap, TN 37327, during 2004**.

Bottom line: start with the USPS boxholder request to the Dunlap Postmaster. It is the cleanest and cheapest legal step. But because this is from 2004, do not be surprised if the record no longer exists.
